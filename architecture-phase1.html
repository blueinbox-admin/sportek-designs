Phase 1 · Internal Team Tool

Sportek AI Internal Dashboard: Architecture

Three data sources, each feeding one chatbot inside a single branded internal website behind a staff login. Every bot is an AI assistant connected to one source only. The bots never share or combine data, which removes the riskiest part of these builds while still delivering the must-have: sales by rep, style, and invoice.

Sportek AI Dashboard internal · staff login · three tabs
Tab 1 Shopify bot
  • Answers the team's everyday questions about the online store: products, inventory, orders, and customers.
  • Connect it securely, behind a staff login, with control over what each person can see.
  • Confirm which storefront or storefronts it should cover.
  • Talk to the team to learn how their store is set up and the questions they ask most.
  • Make sure the bot understands how their data is structured, plus Sportek's own product names, terms, and workflows, so it reads everything the way the team does.
  • Agree on what a good answer looks like (for example, what counts as low stock or a fulfilled order).
  • Keep it read-only and in its lane: it only reads and answers, never changes data, and says when it doesn't know instead of guessing.
  • Train the bot on those questions, then test against real ones and refine until every answer is accurate and never made up.
  • The team signs off before go-live, and the bot is kept current as products and the store change.
Tab 2 QuickBooks bot
  • Answers the sales team's questions about sales, broken down by salesperson, style, and invoice.
  • Connect it securely to the accounting data, behind the same staff login.
  • Works with QuickBooks Online; if Sportek is on the Desktop version, that changes the approach, so it's worth confirming first.
  • A one-time conversation with the reps and accounting to learn how sales are recorded today.
  • Make sure the bot understands how their books are structured, plus Sportek's style codes, internal shorthand, and the way the team talks about sales.
  • Confirm the salesperson is actually captured on each sale, since the whole thing depends on it.
  • Pin down exactly how Sportek counts a sale, after returns, credits, and discounts, and check the totals against their existing reports.
  • Keep it read-only and in its lane: it only reads and answers, never changes the books, and says when it doesn't know instead of guessing.
  • Train and test this one the hardest, since these are the numbers the team will rely on day to day.
  • The team signs off on the numbers before go-live, and the bot is kept current as reps and records change.
Tab 3 HubSpot bot
  • Answers the team's questions about the CRM: contacts, deals, and pipeline.
  • Connect it securely to the CRM, behind the same staff login.
  • If HubSpot isn't populated or used much yet, this bot can move to a later phase.
  • Confirm what data is actually in there and being kept up to date.
  • Map how their records are organized with whoever owns HubSpot, and the questions that matter.
  • Make sure the bot understands their data structure plus the team's own labels and workflow terms, so it reads the CRM the way they do.
  • Keep it read-only and in its lane: it only reads and answers, never changes records, and says when it doesn't know instead of guessing.
  • Train the bot on those questions, then test and refine until the answers hold up.
  • The team signs off before go-live, and the bot is kept current as the CRM changes.
Where the real work is Connecting the data is largely off-the-shelf now: each source plugs in with little custom work. The real work is everything after the connection: learning how each team's data is organized and how they ask questions, encoding that, and testing against real questions until the answers are trustworthy. The QuickBooks bot carries the most of it, since the must-have (sales by rep, after returns, credits, and discounts) is a definition no tool makes for you.
