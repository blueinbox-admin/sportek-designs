Sportek AI Internal Dashboard: Architecture

One branded website behind a staff login, with three AI assistants side by side: Shopify, QuickBooks, and HubSpot. Each is wired to one source and only answers about that source. Keeping them separate keeps it simple and safe, and still delivers the must-have: how much each rep sold, broken down by style, traceable back to the invoices behind it.

Phase 1 is about getting each source right on its own. Every bot answers from a single system, and the team teaches it Sportek's words and rules right in the tool, like what counts as a sale or what a style code means.

What it will do
  • Answer everyday questions in plain English. Ask the way you'd ask a coworker, with no reports or filters to build. For example, "Which products are low on stock?" or "How much did Maria sell last month?"
  • Cover each system's day-to-day. Shopify stock, orders, and customers; QuickBooks sales broken down by salesperson, style, and invoice (the must-have); HubSpot contacts, deals, and pipeline.
  • Learn Sportek's language, two ways. First David interviews the team and programs in the starting rules (style codes, what a sale means). Then, as people use it, they add new words and rules with "remember this" that take effect right away; David reviews them in a weekly pass to make sure they're working as intended.
  • Stay read-only and show its work. It never changes data, and it can point to the records behind an answer so the team can check the numbers themselves.
  • Work anywhere, for the whole team. Just a browser and the staff login, nothing to install, so everyone who should have access can use it from any device.
  • Get sharper over time. As staff add notes with "remember this," it picks up more of Sportek's words and rules and gets more reliable the more the team uses it.
  • Sit behind a staff login. Secure, with control over what each person can see.
What it won't do
  • Combine or map data across the three sources. Each bot only sees its own system, so a question that needs two of them can't be answered. For example, "How many of yesterday's online orders were Maria's?" needs Shopify orders linked to QuickBooks salespeople, which takes real data modeling across systems and gets complex fast, so it's not in scope right now.
  • Change anything, it's read-only. It answers questions but never creates, edits, deletes, refunds, or fulfills. That's by design.
  • Know anything outside the connected system. If it lives in a spreadsheet, an email, a text thread, or someone's head, the bot can't see it.
  • Fill in missing data. Answers are only as good as what's entered. It's built and tested to flag a gap rather than guess, so if the rep isn't recorded on a sale, it's set up to say so instead of inventing one.
  • Teach itself. New words and rules come from the team's notes, not the bot inventing them on its own. David's weekly review keeps what's been added accurate, and the big cross-system lookups still wait for Phase 2.
  • Watch the data or alert you. It answers when asked. No "ping me when stock runs low," it's a question box, not a monitor.

Tab 2 QuickBooks bot (click to see simple mockup)
  • Answers the sales team's questions about sales, broken down by salesperson, style, and invoice. Sliceable by date, rep, style, or customer, and it can show the invoices behind a number.
  • Connected securely behind the staff login; Sportek's financial data stays private and is never used to train outside models.
  • Works with QuickBooks Online; if Sportek is on Desktop, that changes the approach, so confirm first.
  • The core of the build: learning how sales are recorded, confirming the salesperson is captured on each sale, and pinning down exactly how Sportek counts a sale (net of returns, credits, and discounts), checked against their existing reports.
  • Read-only and in its lane: it only reads, never changes the books, and is built to flag what it doesn't know rather than guess.
  • Tested the hardest, since the team relies on these numbers; staff can flag anything off for David to fix.
